A LIFE OF IDEAS

A scientist who
kept looking up.

Born in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, in 1931, A. P. J. Abdul Kalam grew into a scientist whose work connected research with national ambition. He studied physics and aerospace engineering before beginning a career that would span India's space and defence programmes.

At ISRO, Kalam served as project director for the Satellite Launch Vehicle-III. In 1980, the vehicle placed the Rohini satellite into near-Earth orbit, a landmark in India's ability to launch its own satellites. He later worked at DRDO, leading a programme that advanced indigenous guided-missile development.

His public service continued beyond the laboratory. Kalam held senior scientific advisory roles and, in 2002, became India's 11th President. He was known to many as the “People's President,” and he used the office to speak with students about learning, responsibility, and the future.

After his presidency, he returned to teaching, writing, and meeting young people. His life is remembered not only for scientific milestones, but also for the way he encouraged curiosity and service in others.

A LASTING LEGACY

The work continues
in every curious mind.

Kalam's example joined scientific discipline with an open invitation to learn. His story continues to prompt a useful question: what can we build when knowledge is paired with imagination and a sense of responsibility?
